How it works

Shake. It appears. Ask.

  1. 1

    Shake your mouse

    Five quick back-and-forth strokes in under a second. Slow moves and drags are ignored.

  2. 2

    Wiggle appears at your cursor

    Your pointer turns into a rainbow arrow. You are still looking at the same place on screen.

  3. 3

    Pick Chat, Screenshot, Voice, or Workflow

    The keys are C, S, V, and W. Chat, Screenshot, and Voice ask about the screen, with Apple Intelligence, a local model through Ollama, or your own Claude or OpenAI key. Workflow records what you do and replays it.

    • C Chat
    • S Screenshot
    • V Voice
    • W Workflow

What you can do

Three ways to ask.

Chat

Ask in writing about whatever is on screen, without leaving the spot where you got stuck.

Screenshot

Take a screenshot when the question needs the screen. You choose it. It is not taken for you in the background.

Voice

Ask out loud. Voice is a mode you pick, the same way you pick Chat or Screenshot.

Workflow

Record it once

Press Record and the ring around the pointer turns red. Wiggle records clicks, double-clicks, right-clicks, drags, scrolling, typing, and keyboard shortcuts in any app. A small bar shows how many steps are recorded. Esc pauses and resumes. Nothing you do while paused is recorded, and that Esc is not recorded or passed on to the app. Wiggle again, press stop on the bar, or use the menu bar to stop. Then name the workflow, remove any stray steps, and save. Typed text shows as one step, such as Type “hello”.

Play it back

Press play next to a saved workflow and run it 1 to 99 times in a row. It replays the same spots, at the speed you recorded. A small bar shows progress, such as “Step 3 of 14 · run 2 of 5”, and places itself away from the spots the workflow clicks. Esc pauses between steps, and Esc again resumes. A wiggle, or the stop button, stops the run. A run also stops if a display is connected or disconnected, or the Mac goes to sleep. Rename a workflow with the pencil button or by double-clicking the name, or delete it.

Models

Your Mac, a local model, or your own key.

Apple Intelligence

On a Mac that has it, Wiggle can use Apple’s on-device model. That one runs on the Mac.

Ollama

Point Wiggle at a local model you already run through Ollama. That model stays on the machine.

Your Claude or OpenAI key

Bring your own key when you want those models. That question goes to the provider you chose.

Private by design

No account. No analytics.

Wiggle does not ask you to make an account, and the app does not ship with analytics. What you do stays with you. Anything Wiggle sends is listed for you to see. A question you send with your own Claude or OpenAI key goes to that provider, because you asked it to. A workflow stays on your Mac, in ~/Library/Application Support/Wiggle/workflows.json, and is never sent anywhere. macOS withholds keystrokes typed into password fields, so passwords are never recorded.

Questions

Straight answers.

Longer versions of these answers are on the blog.

Shake to open

Move the pointer back and forth five times, quickly, in under a second. A slow drag does not count.

Four choices

Chat, Screenshot, Voice, and Workflow. You pick one each time Wiggle opens. The keys are C, S, V, and W.

Which model

Apple Intelligence, a local model through Ollama, or your own Claude or OpenAI key.

Screenshots

A screenshot happens when you choose Screenshot. The website preview does not capture your screen.

Voice

Voice runs when you choose it. It is not an always-on microphone.

No account

There is no Wiggle account and no analytics in the product we describe. Email hello@jabble.si if you want early access.

Early access

Wiggle is in early access for Mac. Workflow is part of that same app. There is no price on this site.

What permissions does it ask for?

Workflow needs Accessibility. macOS asks for it the first time you record or run a workflow.

What if a workflow goes wrong?

Esc pauses between steps. Esc again resumes. A wiggle, or the stop button, stops the run.

What are the limits?

A workflow replays fixed screen positions, so windows need to be where they were when you recorded. It does not use AI, read the screen, or adapt to changes. Esc cannot be recorded as a step.
